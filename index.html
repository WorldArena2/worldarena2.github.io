Overview

The paper argues that existing embodied world model benchmarks are too narrow: they are mostly vision-only, mostly offline, and mostly simulator-bound. WorldArena 2.0 broadens the benchmark to reflect the requirements of embodied intelligence in the real world.

WorldArena 2.0 is a standardized benchmark for embodied world models that extends the original WorldArena along three coordinated axes: modality, functionality, and platform. It introduces visuo-tactile evaluation, treats world models as online RL environments, and evaluates across RoboTwin 2.0, LIBERO, and the AgileX split-type ALOHA robot. The goal is not only to measure perceptual quality, but also to verify whether world models remain useful when they must support manipulation, policy improvement, and sim-to-real deployment.

Why WorldArena 2.0

The motivation is simple: stronger video prediction does not automatically mean stronger embodied utility. The benchmark therefore pushes evaluation toward richer sensing, closed-loop interaction, and physical deployment.

Functionality Green

  • Reward-aware state transitions
  • Long-horizon robustness under compounding errors

Simulator → Real Robot

Online RL Environment

This is one of the key contributions of the paper: world models are used as interactive RL environments rather than being scored only as passive predictors.

World Model

Predicts future observations conditioned on actions and instructions.

Reward Model

Assigns task-relevant reward signals over imagined rollouts.

GRPO

Uses the imagined environment to optimize policy updates.

Policy Update

Iteratively improves the embodied agent with closed-loop feedback.

Evaluation

Measures success rate on simulated and real-world rollouts.

The RL formulation in the paper views the real environment as a partially observable Markov decision process and asks whether a learned world model can remain stable, action-consistent, and reward-relevant over long interactive trajectories.
